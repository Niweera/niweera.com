the markers

Three domes stand in the old city in the order of the three stars of the hunter's belt — the same order three tombs hold in a desert two thousand miles west. A carving in the stone there is older than any chronicle that should explain it. The markers are instruments. An instrument is read from a place, at a time. Supply both and the instrument reads back.

the vantage

The belt's lead star sits a degree and a half below the celestial equator. From where do you read the markers? Give the latitude and the instrument returns how high that star climbs at its peak.

One vantage puts it three-quarters of the way up the sky; that is the angle the carving was cut for. Another, far to the north, leaves it low. Only one of them is standing where the marker stands.

the seven rings

The carving is seven rings, four marks to a ring, read from the outer ring inward. Four marks are the oldest counting there is. Read them.

ring 2   C A A C
ring 3   C C A G
ring 4   C C A G
ring 5   C A G C
ring 6   C A C C
ring 7   C C A G

Four marks, four values. Four values are two bits. Eight bits are a letter. Seven rings are a word, and the word is what you are.

the moment

The instrument will not finish for a visitor; it finishes for a reader who brings the moment the last reader stopped. You found that moment on the listener's cadence — a broadcast that ran once and was never repeated. Bring it here, to the second.
